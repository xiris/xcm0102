CM Scout Intrinsic and Attributes Intrinsic Value 

Quote 

Post by samsami » Wed Mar 03, 2021 11:53 am 

On the old cm0102 website the author of this incredible Windows 10 app (Vladimir Filatov) introduced his tool, available from the Microsoft Store, as follows: 

I developed a new CM Scout-like app named CM Scout Intrinsic. I developed it for myself, but decided to release the app for everyone. The app works only on Windows 10 and can be downloaded from the Microsoft Store. 

The main feature of the app is the possibility to view intrinsic values and search players based on intrinsic values. 
You can create persistent filters which will be saved between the app launches. 
Also you can create custom weights sets for rating calculation ... and you can have several weights sets and easily switch between them. 

Some notes: 
- only uncompressed saves are currently supported 
- to rename a filter or a weights set please tap on its name at the top of the window 
- you can edit a weights set in the app or export it, edit txt and import back into the app 
- after installing the app, you will see three weights sets: my default weights set, a weights set from CM Scout (excluding weights for current ability, left and right foots which CM Scout also uses) and Mad Scientist's weights set 

Thanks, 
Vladimir

For reference, this is the post from the old forum where vfilatov explained how the CM Scout Intrinsic tool calculates the player rating (%) based on the weight set, I thought it was important reposting it here: 

For each attribute with value v and weight w we calculate the weighted value k as 
k = (v / 20) * w 
Then we summarize all the weighted values and divide by the sum of the weights. 
Below the sample for two attributes. 

Code: Select all 

k = v * w
Passing 9 18/20 10
Long Shots 1 5/20 4
_______________________________________________
 10 / 14 = 0.71 * 100 = 71% 

Note: for Dirtiness and Injury Proneness if real value is v we use 21 - v as v in the formula because а smaller value is better for these attributes.

samsami wrote: Tue Mar 09, 2021 6:58 am I like my central defenders to have good heading skills. You don't give that much weight at all. Why is that? 
In game manual it mentions Heading is "The degree of accuracy that a player has when heading the ball.Heading works in conjunction with the jumping attribute." 

So I think a DC when in defensive duties don't need a good heading, just a good jumping (as it doesnt matter if his header will be accurate, just if he will win the header vs the SC - and to win the header I think he just needs a good jumping). 

However for offensive corners its great to send the DCs to the opponent area because they are tall, so this combined with a good heading accuracy can result in goals for our team. So thats a good idea, I edited my weight set in post above with bigger heading for DCs and also several other tweaks.

Post by samsami » Wed May 12, 2021 9:43 pm 

Like I wrote somewhere else, a number of the players attributes (17 of them, to be exact) have not just one but two values. The value you see in the player's profile on screen (which is always between 1 and 20) and an additional hidden value (the so-called intrinsic value). This means that a player may perform better (or worse) than you'd expect. It's unpredictable how a player will turn out if you don't know his intrinsic scores. 

There is an app for it in the Windows store: CM Scout Intrinsic.

Post by samsami » Sat May 15, 2021 9:15 am 

The nice thing about the CMScout Intrinsic app is that is makes intrinsic values easier to understand/read and to use! 

In the discussion on the old website (see the links above) the instrinsic values are taken from Graeme Kelly's Save Game Editor which can get very confusing as 129 is the worst possible intrinsic value. So the scale goes from 129 - 255 then resets at zero and goes from 0-128 (which is the highest). 

CMScout Intrinsic has multiple view modes, including Intrinsic normalised, in-game values and in-match values. The in-match values are the most interesting for practical purposes. In fact, this may be the only view some of you will want to use, as it shows the values which are actually used by the match engine (which can be higher than 20). So you can instantly see is if a striker with 20 for finishing really does have 20 or perhaps 26 or 28 or even 30 or more. I have a wingback with 32 for crossing!
