Like MadScientist says in the other post, if you want to know what score the match engine uses then use this formula: 

Intrinsic Value/5 + CA/20 + 10 

To use your example, player A has 77 (20) for finishing and CA 70 and player 2 has 44 (18) for finishing and CA 160: 

Player A = 77/5 + 70/20 + 10 = 26,8 
Player B = 44/5 + 160/20 + 10 = 24.1 

(I changed your 20 and 18 for finishing to 77 and 44 because you need to use the GK Intrinsic score, not the in game score)

Post by samsami » Tue Jan 25, 2022 12:13 pm 

The fact that the match engine uses CA/20 in the formula, means that a CA of 100 is only 5 points and a CA of 200 is only 10 points 

So a player's real score as used by the match engine for finishing or position or heading, etc. depends more on his intrinsic_value (which is divided by 5) than on his CA (which is divided by 20) 

Theoretical maximum score for Finishing (and other intrinsics) would then be (125/5) + (200/20) + 10 which amounts to 25 + 10 + 10 = 45 
My best player currently has 77/5 + 70/20 + 10 which amounts to 15.4 + 3.5 + 10 - 28.9 for Finishing (but that's in the Second Division) 

Now those formulas are used by the CM Scout Intrinsic app which in my case shows that the match engine indeed uses 28 for finishing with my striker. 
So you don't even have to calculate all of this yourself.

One reason I think the CM Scout Intrinsic app is such an invaluable tool is because the Intrinsic values are far more important than Current Ability (CA) and Potential Ability (PA) which seem vastly overrated to me. 

When you have two players both with the highest possible intrinsic score (i.e., 125) for finishing or crossing or long shots, or whatever, while the first player has a CA of 1 and the second player has a CA of 200 the difference of their attribute's in-match value (which is what really matters when you play a game) is only 10 points when you use the MadScientist's formula or check the in-match value in the CM Scout Intrinsic app. 

Player 1 = 125/5 + 1/20 + 10 = 35 (in-match value) 
Player 2 = 125/5 + 200/20 + 10 = 45 (in-match value) 

That's why I say that CA and PA are overrated. CA only increases the in-match value by a maximum of 10 points (200/20) while the intrinsic score can increase the in-match value by a maximum of 25 points (125/5) 

That's why you can have a star player (like player 1 above) who has several attributes with a score anywhere between 20 and 35 but a PA of <10 (like I do). 

(By the way, CA does have some additional value in that players with a high CA tend to have more attributes with high intrinsic scores than players with a low CA. Several of my players at English Division Two level have two or three attributes with a 20+ score but Premier League players with high PA will have several more.)
