## Reliability model

Use the official manual as the primary authority for exposed game systems: screens, actions, manager duties, player attributes, training categories, tactics, scouting, transfers, match day, board interactions, news, and network play.

Use forums/articles as community evidence for how players discovered the game actually behaves: dominant tactics, key attributes, training routines, exploits, hidden mechanics, famous players, and community norms. These sources sometimes disagree; preserve that uncertainty instead of pretending one formula is definitive.

Use reviews/context pieces for product feel: speed, accessibility, database depth, nostalgia, technical friction, and why the game outlived its era.

## Known gaps

Some public pages blocked automated scraping during this pass: StrategyWiki, GameFAQs, and Fandom. The core concepts from those sources are covered by the official manual and community sources, but a later browser/manual pass could add them.

## One-paragraph research conclusion

CM0102 works because it combines a huge credible football database, fast progression, readable text/table UI, enough tactical and transfer agency to create stories, and enough mystery in attributes/match mechanics to fuel community experimentation. A modern online version should preserve speed, clarity, data depth, tactical/recruitment fun, moddability, and emergent career drama, while improving onboarding, multiplayer progression, UI/search quality, installation/reliability, data updates, and exploit balance.
